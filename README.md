# EMPIRICAL ANALYSIS ON VOLATILITY AND PROBABILITIES: A STUDY ON OPTIONS AND PREDICTION MARKETS

- **Hypothesis 1 : Price Consistency Across Markets**

Analyzing price formation across different types of markets is crucial for understanding how information is integrated. This hypothesis aims to verify whether financial instruments in two distinct markets produce consistent or divergent signals. The objective is to evaluate how the prices of an event in a prediction market and binary options prices are reflected.

"Given an event that is identically defined in both the financial and prediction market, the prices of contracts representing equivalent outcomes should exhibit a relationship"


- **Hypothesis 2 : Implied Volatility Alignment**

Implied volatility is a widely used metric in the financial world. However, it varies depending on the nature of the market or the pricing mechanisms. It is therefore important to determine whether estimates derived from the options market are equivalent to those obtained from prediction markets.

"Implied volatility derived from options markets should be equivalent to the one derived from prediction markets for a same event"


- **Hypothesis 3 : Probability Density Functions Similitude**

Beyond prices and the volatility involved, the complete structure of market expectations can be understood through its probability density function. This approach provides a more rigorous view of expectations across different types of markets.

"The probability density functions inferred from prediction markets and options markets should reveal similar characteristics"
